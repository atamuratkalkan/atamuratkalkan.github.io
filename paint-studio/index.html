    A practical starting point

    Begin

    Choose a reference, enable the paints you actually own, and generate 3–8 stages. Mark a small focal feature before generating if it needs protection. Tap any stage region for a matching target and estimated recipe. Use point sampling for a region's exact displayed colour, or small-area averaging for a transition.

    What a stage means

    The plan is an edge-aware hierarchy of connected forms. Each later stage subdivides earlier shapes. It is a painterly simplification, not object recognition, a blurred photo, a transparent overlay or a recovery of an original painting's layers. Focal protection biases the hierarchy; it cannot guarantee recognition of an eye. Merge only touching regions. A merge is carried through the hierarchy; undo regenerates the plan.

    Where the colour comes from

    Original picks sample the retained sRGB reference. Stage picks sample the stage colours, including the tonal or ground-colour foundation. Values converts both the visible image and the sampled target to linear-light luminance. New detail shows actual stage changes with earlier context; picks still use the unmuted current-stage target. Small-area samples average linear sRGB, not gamma-encoded bytes.

    What “parts” means

    We use locally vendored Spectral.js 3.0.0: estimated spectral reflectance and a single-constant Kubelka–Munk model, not RGB averaging. Recipes search one, two and three enabled tubes, including white. Ratios are simplified first and then the predicted colour is recalculated and scored in OKLab. “ΔE OK” is a screen colour distance, not a probability or a physical-accuracy percentage.

    Approximate parts are relative model factors, not measured grams or millilitres. Spectral.js converts them to optical concentrations using factor² × estimated strength² × luminance. Website swatches and neutral strength of 1 are unmeasured assumptions. Catalogue references are not proof of your tube’s brand or formulation. Real pigments, brand, knife loading, scattering, ground, illumination and drying can change the result. Test a small pile and adjust. Recorded pigment codes, transparency and future calibration records are not currently used as measured spectra.

    Painting materials

    Visual stages are not automatically dried coats. Wet-on-wet, separate-session and mixed guidance never sets universal drying times or medium doses. Follow the materials manufacturer's guidance for actual paint films. Ground-colour foundation is only an approximate tonal target, not a physical glazing simulation.

    Your files

    JPEG, PNG and WebP only, up to 80 MB and 100 MP. Browser orientation is respected. The oriented reference is retained at up to 2200 px on the long edge; analysis uses up to 512 px. Canvas conversion/output is sRGB. Transparent areas are placed on warm paper. For reliable colour, provide an sRGB source. No EXIF, brand measurements or cloud services are needed.

    Save locally uses a separate Paint Studio IndexedDB database. Export a project for a portable backup including the image, inventory, stages and colour notes. Importing replaces the current workspace; save first if necessary.
